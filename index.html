12. Adversarial Analyst

Growth remains modest: the current 5.5% yield is attractive, but AFFO growth in the 3%–4% range does not automatically produce a 10% total return when terminal valuation is normalized.

Capital-market dependence: net-lease REIT growth depends on the spread between acquisition yields and the cost of debt/equity. Realty Income raised equity through ATM forwards and continues issuing debt; adverse capital-market pricing can slow accretion.

Interest-rate sensitivity: a high-quality REIT still competes with bonds and other income vehicles. Higher long rates can compress P/AFFO even if property operations remain healthy.

Platform expansion introduces complexity: data centers, private capital, credit and JVs diversify growth but add underwriting and execution risks beyond the traditional single-tenant retail model.

Counterweight: the bear case is not an operating-collapse thesis. Occupancy, rent recapture, credit quality, diversification and dividend coverage remain strong enough that valuation discipline—not business deterioration—drives the WAIT verdict.
